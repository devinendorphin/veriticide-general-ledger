## 1. *Rona v. Trump* (S.D.N.Y., Judge Jesse Furman) — permanent injunction

Ruling date **30 Jul 2025**; final judgment **18 Aug 2025**. Both dates come from search results
(Foley Hoag; Clearinghouse) and are not captured. **Scope:** the two plaintiffs only. The order
was not struck down on its face.

> Judge Jesse Furman, U.S. District Judge in the Southern District of New York ruled that Executive
> Order 14203—which authorized sweeping sanctions against the ICC and draconian civil penalties
> against those who support its work—violates the plaintiffs’ right to engage in
> constitutionally-protected speech.

> While the immediate trigger for the Executive Order was the ICC’s decision to issue arrest
> warrants against senior Israeli officials as part of its Palestine investigation, the sanctions
> effectively undermine accountability efforts across all the ICC’s workstreams, including those
> that the U.S. government has supported—for example in Ukraine and Darfur.

*The second paragraph is OSJI's own characterization, as co-counsel. It is not the court's
finding.*

## 2. *Smith v. Trump* (D. Me.) — motion to dismiss denied, 28 Sep 2026

> The September 28 ruling allows the challenge brought by Fortify Rights founder and CEO Matthew
> Smith and international human rights lawyer Akila Radhakrishnan to proceed.

> “In my work, I help the ICC investigate hideous crimes committed against the people of Myanmar,
> including mass murder, torture, and human trafficking,” said Matthew Smith […].

> The lawsuit challenges Executive Order 14203, issued on February 6, 2025, which threatens
> penalties for communications with the ICC’s Office of the Prosecutor, including sharing evidence,
> expert analysis, and legal advice.

> The ruling is not a final decision on the legality of the restrictions. However, the court found a
> strong likelihood that the executive order violated the plaintiffs’ First Amendment rights. It
> also rejected the administration’s arguments that the plaintiffs lacked standing and that their
> claims were premature.

> A July 2025 preliminary injunction continues to prohibit the administration from punishing Smith
> and Radhakrishnan for their communications with the ICC while the case proceeds.

## Why this item matters (A1)

These are the only findings in the packet made by a **neutral adjudicator** about the character of
the conduct. Three federal judges, in three districts, have each found the order likely or actually
unconstitutional **as applied to speech**: communications, evidence-sharing, advice, and
recommendations to the Court. That is an independent, non-proposer basis for reading part of the
conduct as a penalty on testimony (Convention Art. II(2)(d)). It is narrower than this packet's
charge. The courts ruled on U.S. constitutional rights of U.S.-connected plaintiffs, not on
international law and not on the institutional designation.

## BOUNDARY

**Establishes (as reported):** three federal rulings adverse to the government's application of
E.O. 14203 to speech. One is a permanent injunction, one a preliminary injunction, and one a denial
of a motion to dismiss with a finding of likely First Amendment violation.

**Does not establish alone:** the order's facial invalidity, any appellate outcome, or anything
about the 9 Oct 2026 institutional designation, which post-dates all three rulings and was not
before any of these courts. Sources 1 and 2 are interested parties. The opinions must be captured
before these findings are relied on at P1.
