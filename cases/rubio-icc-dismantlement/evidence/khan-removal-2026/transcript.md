## 1. JURIST, verbatim excerpts

> The Assembly of States Parties to the International Criminal Court (ICC) voted Friday to remove
> Chief Prosecutor Karim Khan from office over sexual misconduct allegations from 2024. Khan’s
> lawyer, Tayab Ali, denounced the vote in a statement. He said that the investigation found no
> misconduct, including sexual misconduct, against Khan. Ali’s statement also claimed that Khan was
> not permitted to make submissions and was excluded from the session, pledging to challenge the
> decision.

> Assembly President Päivi Kaukoranta announced that 82 of the 125 states parties found that Khan
> committed serious misconduct and a serious breach of duty. Kaukoranta countered Ali’s claims,
> stating that the assembly fully respected Khan’s due process and that the investigation did find
> evidence supporting the misconduct allegations. The Assembly’s oversight body commissioned a UN
> Office of Internal Oversight Services investigation into the allegations, which ran for over a
> year. It centered on a former aide who said Khan subjected her to non-consensual sexual acts. Khan
> has consistently denied any wrongdoing. His removal is the first of a sitting chief prosecutor in
> the court’s 24-year history.

> Khan and his supporters have framed the disciplinary process as political retaliation for the
> Israeli warrants. However, Palestinian representatives have rejected that characterization.

> Khan’s dismissal does not affect the cases against Netanyahu and Gallant, because only ICC judges
> can withdraw warrants.

## Date

JURIST says "Friday." The ABC (Australia) headline is dated 2026-07-25. The removal vote is
therefore dated **Friday 24 Jul 2026**. This is an inference from the two dates and is marked for
confirmation against the ASP record.

## BOUNDARY

**Establishes:** that the Court's own oversight system removed its chief prosecutor for "serious
misconduct and a serious breach of duty" by a vote of 82 of 125 states parties. It also records an
earlier, conflicting judges'-panel report (reported second-hand) and the prosecutor's denial. **This
cuts in both directions.** It gives the State Department's "corrupt" characterization a partial
factual footing as to one officer. It is also evidence that the institution has a working internal
accountability mechanism, which cuts against "unaccountable."

**Does not establish alone:** that any judicial decision of the Court was affected by the
misconduct. The warrants were issued by judges, not the prosecutor. It does not establish whether
the removal process was fair; Khan's counsel disputes it. It does not establish anything about the
other designated officials.

public sources give. This item's classification language is aimed at institutions and moves, never
