## 1. Duterte: charges confirmed, 23 April 2026 — verbatim

> Today, 23 April 2026, Pre-Trial Chamber I of the International Criminal Court (“ICC” or “the
> Court”) unanimously confirmed all the charges comprised of three counts of crimes against
> humanity brought by the Prosecutor against Rodrigo Roa Duterte and committed him to trial before
> a Trial Chamber.
>
> Pre-Trial Chamber I concluded that there are substantial grounds to believe that Mr Duterte is
> responsible for the crimes against humanity of murder and attempted murder, pursuant to article
> 7(1)(a) of the Rome Statute (ICC’s founding treaty). These crimes were committed as part of a
> widespread and systematic attack against a civilian population on the territory of the Republic
> of the Philippines between 1 November 2011 and 16 March 2019 in the context of the “war on drugs”
> campaign.

> Pre-Trial Chamber I based its decision on the evidence and arguments presented by the Prosecutor
> and the Defence during the confirmation of charges hearing from 23 to 27 February 2026, as well as
> on the submissions and documents of the parties and participants, including participating victims.

*The trial date (30 Nov 2026) comes from secondary search results only. It is not in the captured
release and is not relied on.*

## 2. Darfur: Abd-Al-Rahman ("Ali Kushayb") case page — verbatim

> On 6 October 2025, Trial Chamber I found Ali Muhammad Ali Abd-Al-Rahman guilty of 27 counts of
> crimes against humanity and war crimes, in Darfur, Sudan, between August 2003 and April 2004.
>
> On 9 December, Trial Chamber I sentenced Mr Abd-Al-Rahman to 20 years of imprisonment. The time
> spent in detention, from 9 June 2020 onwards, will be deducted from his sentence. Mr Abd-Al-Rahman
> will remain in the ICC detention centre until the ICC designates a country to serve his sentence.
>
> Next steps: On 6 November 2025, the Defence of Mr Abd-Al-Rahman filed its notice of appeal
> against the judgment in the case. The Defence and Prosecution filed their notices of appeal
> against the sentence on 8 and 9 January 2026, respectively. The Appeals Chamber will rule on these
> appeals in due course. On 9 December 2025, Trial Chamber I issued an order for submissions on
> reparations, with the relevant timeline and guidance for this process in this case.

> The trial in the case opened before Trial Chamber I on 5 April 2022. 56 witnesses appeared in the
> courtroom during the presentation of the Prosecution’s case […]. On 5 June 2023, the common Legal
> Representatives of Victims made an opening statement.

## Analyst note (A1)

- Both proceedings are **live**: Duterte awaits trial, and Abd-Al-Rahman's appeals and reparations
  are pending. Neither involves a U.S. or Israeli national, and neither man is a "protected person"
  under E.O. 14203 §8(d).
- Both men are **ICC detainees**. The 9 Oct 2026 fact sheet issues a general licence "authorizing
  certain activity related to the ICC detainees." That suggests the designation's drafters modeled
  its effect on ongoing custody. This is an inference from the licence structure; the licence
  text (OFAC) is not captured.
- The Darfur case is the strongest closed record on the Court's docket: a conviction **beyond
  reasonable doubt**, now on appeal, with reparations to victims pending. An institution-wide
  designation reaches that reparations process too.

## BOUNDARY

**Establishes:** that the Court has live proceedings, including a conviction with reparations
pending, that concern neither the U.S. nor Israel and fall outside the E.O.'s protected class.

**Does not establish alone:** that the 9 Oct 2026 designation has impaired any of these
proceedings. The general licences defer most effects for 180 days, and the detainee licence
expressly carves out custody activity.
