## Analyst notes (A1)

- **Withdrawals: causation not established.** The Sahel states' stated grounds (Global-South
  selectivity) **predate** the July 2026 campaign (announced Sep 2025) and are a long-standing
  critique of the Court in their own right. Venezuela's stated ground is "geographical bias"
  (search results; not captured). An ICC investigation into the Venezuelan government's own
  crackdowns has been open since 2021 (analyst knowledge; not captured). The Chad-to-US-request link is "reportedly." **None of the
  five withdrawals is attributable to the campaign on this record.**
- **The 2022 Senate resolution** (quoted by the NYC Bar, S1; the resolution text is not captured)
  is the asymmetry anchor. Russia is a non-party to the Rome Statute, and the U.S. Senate
  unanimously supported the Court investigating Russian nationals on the territory of a non-party
  (Ukraine, by Art. 12(3) declaration). The Court's jurisdiction over non-party nationals was
  endorsed when the accused were adversaries' nationals. It is contested as illegitimate when they
  are U.S. or allied nationals. That is a different administration (2022) and a different branch
  (the Senate). The asymmetry is at the level of the U.S. state over time, not a claim about
  Rubio's personal consistency. *Rubio was a sitting senator in 2022. Whether he was among the
  unanimous consent is not established (U; owed: the Congressional Record entry).*

## BOUNDARY

**Establishes:** the civil-society record of warnings. It establishes the withdrawals as announced
facts. It establishes the 2022 Senate resolution as reported by the NYC Bar.

**Does not establish alone:** that any withdrawal was caused by U.S. pressure. It does not
establish that the NYC Bar's legal conclusion ("not consistent with United States law") is
correct. That is a contested legal position, and the American Servicemembers' Protection Act of
2002 points the other way on several questions. It does not establish the Senate resolution's
exact text.
