## 1. UN News, verbatim excerpts

> Mr. Guterres was “deeply alarmed” by the expected impact on the court’s “continued ability to
> perform its important mandates” and called the sanctions “a serious blow to our collective
> pursuit of international criminal justice, of which the ICC is a key pillar”.

> Independent UN human rights experts warned separately that the US sanctions were a “brash
> attempt” to dismantle a collective international legal system to hold perpetrators of the gravest
> crimes to account.

> “Sanctioning the institution entrusted with delivering justice for the gravest international
> crimes is an assault on the foundations of international accountability and rule of law,” the
> experts said.
>
> “No State should place itself beyond the reach of the law by intimidating the court established
> to enforce it and attempting to dismantle it.”

> The UN Human Rights Council-appointed experts – who are not employed by the UN and receive no
> salary for their work – added that the attack “directly injures the victims of atrocities and
> their advocates, who rely on the ICC for justice, accountability and reparation”.

## BOUNDARY

**Establishes:** that on the day of the designation the UN Secretary-General, the High
Commissioner for Human Rights, HRC-appointed experts, and the foreign ministers of eight U.S.
allies or partners warned of its effect on the Court's work. It also records that the High
Commissioner himself noted the six-month delay and that "service providers can continue to work."
That is counter-evidence against reading the designation as an immediate shutdown.

**Does not establish alone:** any realized effect. Every statement here is a forecast or a
characterization. Several speakers have institutional interests (the UN's relationship agreement
with the Court; states parties' commitments; advocacy organizations).
