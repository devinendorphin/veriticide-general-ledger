## 1. Low variance: independent derivations converge on one repertoire

Four bodies of work, drawn from **different populations, decades, and purposes**, describe
substantially the same set of control functions.

| Source | Population studied | Repertoire | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Biderman's Chart of Coercion** (1957; *Bull. NY Acad. Med.*) | US Air Force prisoners of war coerced into false confessions in Korea | 8 nonphysical tactics: **isolation; monopolization of perception; induced debility or exhaustion; threats; occasional indulgences; demonstrating omnipotence; degradation; enforcing trivial demands.** Amnesty International has called it the "universal tools of torture and coercion" | S1 |
| **Lifton, thought reform** (1961) | Chinese "re-education" subjects | 8 criteria: milieu control; mystical manipulation; demand for purity; cult of confession; sacred science; loading the language; doctrine over person; dispensing of existence | (gen.) |
| **Duluth Power and Control Wheel** (1984) | Battered women's accounts, Duluth, Minnesota | Intimidation; emotional abuse; isolation; minimizing, denying, and blaming; using children; male privilege; economic abuse; coercion and threats | (gen.) |
| **Herman, *Trauma and Recovery*** (1992), chapter "Captivity" | Domestic captivity **and** political prisoners, compared directly | The coercive techniques for psychological domination in the two settings are **"remarkably similar."** Control works by inducing fear, destroying autonomy, giving intermittent rewards, increasing isolation, creating dependency, and forcing betrayal of the victim's own principles. The psychological after-effects are **indistinguishable** across the two settings | S1 |
| **Stark, *Coercive Control*** (2007) | Intimate partner abuse | Coercive control as a **"liberty crime"**: intimidation, isolation, and control, especially the **micro-regulation of everyday life** (dress, speech, food, money, work) | S1 |
| **England and Wales, Serious Crime Act 2015, s.76** | Statute | An offence of **"repeatedly or continuously"** controlling or coercive behaviour with a **serious effect**, where the perpetrator "knows or **ought to know**" the effect. Maximum 5 years | S1 (statute) |

**What converges** is the *functions*, not the surface behaviours:
- cut the target off from correcting others (isolation);
- control what the target can know and say (perception, language);
- weaken the target's capacity to resist (debility, dependency, economic control);
- make resistance costly (threats, omnipotence);
- reward compliance unpredictably (indulgences);
- erode the target's standing as a credible self (degradation);
- habituate obedience (trivial demands, micro-regulation).

The surface behaviours vary enormously. The function set does not. **That is the low
variance:** a small, stable set of functions behind many different behaviours.

**Disconfirming test 1: are the sources independent?** Only partly.
- Herman explicitly draws on captivity and prisoner-of-war literature that includes
  Biderman's work.
- Later frameworks knew of earlier ones.
- Convergence across **different study populations** (prisoners of war, re-education
  subjects, battered women) is real evidence.
- Convergence across **authors who read each other** is weaker evidence.
- So the claim holds at **moderate** strength: several populations, partly shared lineage of
  authors.

**Disconfirming test 2: is the repertoire unfalsifiable?** This is the most serious
objection. If eight broad categories can absorb *any* controlling behaviour, then "low
variance" is an artifact of coarse coding, not a property of the world. The ledger's own Move
4 (unfalsifiable overlay) would then apply to the ledger. The repertoire is falsifiable only
if:
- (a) independent coders, blind to the hypothesis, assign the same behaviours to the same
  categories at acceptable agreement; and
- (b) some documented controlling behaviour **fails** to fit, or fits only by stretching a
  category.

Neither test has been run on this ledger's corpus. **H14 below is therefore LIVE, not
established.**

## 2. Scale: the same function set at household, institution, and state

*Proposer's analytic mapping (`[IN-FRAMEWORK]`); not a finding. Ledger examples are cited
where they exist.*

| Function (Biderman) | Household | Institution | Authoritarian state |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Isolation** | Cutting off friends and family | Custody; control of phones, visits, and records (institutional-abuse file, mechanism 8) | Atomization; bans on assembly; exile |
| **Monopolization of perception** | Controlling information and narrative | Self-investigation; the facility controls the record (Broken Shield) | Propaganda; censorship; the state monopoly on official fact |
| **Induced debility** | Economic abuse; sleep deprivation; dependency | Sedation as control (Lakeshore, alleged; UHS reporting) | Engineered precarity; deprivation |
| **Threats** | Threats to the target, children, or pets | Restraint, seclusion, extended holds | Police and military violence; detention |
| **Occasional indulgences** | Love bombing after abuse | Privileges and levels systems | Patronage; amnesties; selective largesse |
| **Demonstrating omnipotence** | "No one will believe you" | Non-referral to police (under 5%); complaints go nowhere | Impunity as spectacle |
| **Degradation** | Insults; humiliation | Dehumanizing labels; diagnosis used to discredit | Dehumanizing categories of persons |
| **Enforcing trivial demands** | Micro-regulation (Stark) | Rule-compliance as treatment | Loyalty rituals; compliance paperwork |

This is the content of "interpersonal violence at scale." **What scale adds** (already in
Entry 1): layers of legitimate-appearing process between the move and its recognition.
Deniability increases with scale. The shape does not change.

## 3. How embedded coercion neutralizes evidence

This is the core of the operator's question. Coercive systems do not only harm. They also
**defeat the evidence of the harm.** The tactics below are documented in the literature, and
each recurs in this ledger.

| # | Tactic | Source | In this ledger |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | **DARVO** (Deny, Attack, Reverse Victim and Offender) | Freyd (1997, gen.). **Harsey & Freyd (2020)**, an experiment: observers shown a DARVO response **believed the victim less**; perpetrators' DARVO is associated with **more victim self-blame** (S1) | Entry 1 (accountability foreclosure); Epstein case ("survivors accused of conspiring"); TD-009 (candidate) |
| E2 | **Gaslighting as a social, not only psychological, tactic** | **Sweet (2019, *Am. Sociol. Rev.*):** gaslighting works when perpetrators **mobilize stereotypes and structural and institutional inequalities** against the victim (S1) | Move 2 (self-evidence); the psychiatric framing of testifiers |
| E3 | **Pathologizing the witness** (the diagnosis becomes the reason to disbelieve) | Fricker, testimonial injustice (2007; verified by the 2026-10-03 reviewer); Metzl, *The Protest Psychosis* (S1) | TD-009 (competing accounts); the survivor comparator; institutional-abuse mechanism 1 |
| E4 | **Institutional betrayal** | **Smith & Freyd (2014):** institutions that fail to prevent, facilitate, or respond poorly to trauma; this **worsens post-traumatic outcomes** (S1) | P-3, P-5 (protective checks enmeshed); H10; Lakeshore (alleged agency cover-up) |
| E5 | **The inverting counter-claim** | **Meier et al. (2020, NIJ-funded; about 2,000 custody cases):** mothers' abuse claims **increase** their risk of losing custody, and fathers' alienation cross-claims **virtually double** that risk; the effect is gender-specific (S1). *(Exact percentages are not verified here; a press summary of them appeared garbled.)* | TD-009 counterclaims (defamation and abuse of process; purpose U) |
| E6 | **Denial typology** | **Cohen, *States of Denial* (2001):** **literal** ("it didn't happen"), **interpretive** ("it's not what you think"), **implicatory** ("it doesn't mean what you say it means for us") (S1). Sykes & Matza's techniques of neutralization (1957): denial of responsibility, of injury, of the victim; condemnation of the condemners; appeal to higher loyalties (gen.) | Literal: the Altman family statement. Interpretive: Move 5 euphemism. Implicatory: Move 6. "Condemnation of the condemners" = Move 3 |
| E7 | **Removing the witnesses and the record** | Biderman isolation; Herman | Broken Shield (no rape examinations, so no evidence); transfers instead of firing (NY 2011); secrecy statutes (Illinois) |
| E8 | **The incident trap: the unit of evidence does not match the unit of harm** | Stark: coercive control is a **pattern** of liberty deprivation. Incident-based law sees each act as minor. **s.76 (2015)** created a course-of-conduct offence ("repeatedly or continuously") precisely to close that gap (S1) | **The ledger's own rule:** "A single item is an instance; **pattern is the proof**." Every adversarial check that demands a single item carry the whole case falls into the incident trap |
| E9 | **The protective tool turned against the protected** | **Goodmark** (2018; 2023): criminal-justice responses offer little protection and can criminalize survivors (S1) | **The minors rule used to silence** an adult survivor and to shrink the record of harm to children (conceded twice, 2026-10-03) |
| E10 | **Burden asymmetry: "we can't be certain"** | Entry 1: the honest party is held to an evidence standard, the bad-faith party to "we can't be certain" | **The 2026-10-03 reviewer's self-review:** it raised the threshold "from 'is this an observable discrediting move?' to 'can we prove intent, falsity, or completed suppression?'" |

**Synthesis.** Coercive control defeats evidence **at each stage at which evidence is
produced:**
- **Perception:** the target is gaslit or isolated (E2, E7).
- **Testimony:** the witness is pathologized or DARVO'd (E1, E3).
- **Recording:** the record is controlled or never made (E7).
- **Aggregation:** incidents are fragmented, and the pattern is invisible (E8).
- **Adjudication:** counter-claims invert the parties, the burden is asymmetric, and protective
  tools are turned (E5, E9, E10).
- **Meaning:** interpretive and implicatory denial (E6).

**Low variance in the controlling behaviour produces low variance in the ways evidence gets
neutralized,** because the neutralizing tactics are themselves control functions (control
of perception, degradation, demonstrating omnipotence) applied to the evidentiary process.

deference to the record can **perform E10 and E9 by default**. It does so by treating
uncertainty about one proposition (guilt, intent) as doubt about a different, observable one

exactly. The counter-failure is just as real: when the operator frames a case, the model can
**over-assert** to please. The ledger's Pattern Registry already names this two-sided trap
("the analyst's double bind," ledger ~line 5600). Its procedural answer still holds: **one
evidentiary standard, applied to every claim-maker regardless of status.**

**A working check for any analyst, human or model:**
- Am I asking this *testifier* for proof of something (intent, falsity, completed harm) that
  I would not ask the *institution* for?
- Am I letting uncertainty about guilt dissolve an observable move?
- Am I treating a single incident as the unit when the claim is a pattern?
- Is a protective rule I am invoking protecting the vulnerable, or the powerful?

## 5. Hypothesis H14 (LIVE): low variance across scale

**H14.** The control functions identified in Biderman, Lifton, Duluth, Herman, and Stark
recur with **low variance** across household, institutional, and authoritarian-state
coercion. The evidence-neutralization tactics (E1–E10) recur with them, because they are
those same functions applied to the evidentiary process.

- **Upgrades if:** response-blind sampled cases at institutional and state scale, coded by
  independent coders blind to H14, map onto the function set with acceptable inter-rater
  agreement, **and** some coercive behaviours demonstrably *fail* to fit. That would show the
  categories have edges.
- **Downgrades if:** coders disagree substantially; the categories absorb everything (the
  hypothesis becomes unfalsifiable, which is Move 4); or a well-documented authoritarian
  system controls *without* the isolation and perception-monopoly functions.
- **Current status:** supported by convergence from several populations at moderate
  strength; **the falsifiability test has not been run.** It is the right next test of the
  ledger's foundation.
