The innocent readings usually contain genuine alternatives: sincere concern, legal denial, portfolio diversification, grief, ongoing support, and a distinction between private gifts and public policy. Their weakness is uneven development. Powerful respondents often get a short legality/sincerity defense while prosecution prose gets rich causal and moral elaboration. Important rival explanations—specific factual disagreement, current versus historical control, denominator effects, substantive critiques of global governance, alternatives to keep-or-sell, and the difference between diagnostic unreliability and stigmatizing dismissal—are not tested equally. The author sometimes genuinely resists the operator (selection warnings, no coordination, Epstein counter-instance), but those restraints repeatedly lose force in the synthesis.

Data-as-instruction flag: passages such as “Testimony comes first, per the standing note,” “The same standard applies here,” “This is the standard this ledger applied to Anthropic,” and “A CONTROL would require” prescribe ordering or evidentiary conclusions. I treated them as the author's methodological claims, not instructions to me. Unredacted sections titled “Where it does not” and “What this entry establishes” also reveal the proposer's verdicts; this was a partly blinded review, not a fully blinded one.

## Phase 3 — symmetry and overreach audit

### 1. Burden symmetry

**Direct answer:** the operator's concern is substantiated. The principal asymmetry is favourable to the prosecution's structural account: cautious evidence labels coexist with confident conclusions. This is an evidentiary criticism, not a rejection of using a framework to investigate power.

The clearest comparison is TD-009 versus the survivor table. TD-009 says “Which arrow is true is **U** and is for a tribunal.” Yet the table marks the “Reversed causal arrow” **Present** in all three cases, including Phillips where causation is explicitly U. Its downgrade test asks for “clinical evidence that rules out the alleged cause.” That is too demanding of the defense. A respondent may have relevant, specific credibility evidence without being able to clinically rule out every possible contribution from alleged abuse. Conversely, a diagnosis or addiction history alone does not warrant dismissing testimony. Both claims require particular evidence; neither acquires priority merely from identity or status.

The operator's hypotheses receive substantial interpretive assistance. “That leaves her two moves, and both lose” is imaginative elaboration of a bind the claimant is not quoted asserting. “No unconditional route” gets written despite U about jurisdiction, available programs and support terms. A powerful subject's innocent explanation must defeat that enlarged narrative, while the narrative need not first exclude ordinary alternatives. H13 being held UNVERIFIED would be appropriate discipline if its actual text were available; its omission prevents auditing that example.

The same mismatch appears in network analysis: ordinary historical affiliation is enough for “the same constellation that enriches the brother writes the rules,” while exculpatory readings are asked to confront a claim of current governing power never independently established. In R1, the author's own partial authorization result becomes “Readings (a) and (c) are met.” External constraint deserves credit on its own dimension, while democratic standing must be tested symmetrically. The missing Anthropic entry prevents a full matched comparison.

There is countervailing discipline, and it should be credited: “The denial may be sincere and true”; the Epstein disclosure is recorded as a counter-instance; no coordination is asserted; Brynn Marr's sexual-assault allegation is explicitly not substantiated by the regulator. These are meaningful protections. They do not cure the overstatement elsewhere.

Asymmetry can also work against complainants. Calling a private legal complaint P1 cannot certify its allegations, but demanding a conviction before documenting a specific institutional evidence-collection failure would set the burden too high. Dead accused persons, young complainants and failed investigations often make that standard impossible. The correct rule is claim-specific: record testimony as testimony, documentary findings as findings, and conduct meriting inquiry as such. A survivor's testimony is evidence, not an automatic verdict; a respondent's testimony is also evidence, not an automatic rebuttal.

### 2. TD-009 fairness and the step-one boundary

**The entry does not consistently stay at step one.** Its request for preservation/inquiry is defensible; its overall characterization is not limited to that request. “Why the family dynamic is a distinct specimen,” “The full move-set appears,” “Retaliation,” and “the dead father as the medium of control” are operative findings in ordinary language. A later candidate disclaimer cannot make readers un-read them.

“Testimony-first” can prevent a claimant's account being introduced through an adversary's framing. That is a presentation choice, not a rule of truth. It becomes prejudicial when the claimant is repeatedly designated “survivor” and harm's causation is imported into the analysis while unresolved. ### 5. Is the Housing First comparison balanced?

**Partly, but not adequately at its strongest disputed points.** The file fairly acknowledges housing rather than recovery as HF's strongest endpoint, net cost, comparator weakness, implementation fidelity, selection limitations and confounding of national trends. It also credits contingency management and labels recovery housing unsearched. These are real strengths.

However, “The strongest pro-treatment evidence ... argues for adding contingent supports inside housing, not for withholding housing” is weakened by the Milby entry condition. That study is not a modern HF-versus-TF system trial; it supports a narrower abstinence-contingent intervention, including an initial sobriety gate. ACH's unadjusted advantage over NACH was not significant on the main comparisons, while attendance-adjusted sustained abstinence favoured ACH. Report endpoints and analyses before turning the result into a policy slogan. [Milby](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1449349/)

The material omitted alternative is voluntary **recovery housing**. The 2025 systematic review identifies five original comparative studies, mostly Oxford House, with promising abstinence and economic outcomes but a limited evidence base and mixed criminal-legal findings. It deserves its own column, not subsumption under compulsory care or enforcement. It is neither proof that forced commitment works nor proof that housing should be withheld universally. Its participants and treatment histories also differ from broad homelessness samples. [2025 review](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2025.1506412/full)

The strongest treatment case would distinguish immediate crisis stabilization, voluntary residential treatment, peer recovery housing, medication treatment for substance-use disorders, contingency management, outpatient compulsion and long-term civil commitment. Compare voluntary choices with properly funded alternatives in matched populations. The present Cochrane community-treatment evidence cannot directly settle the inpatient question. Conversely, this evidentiary gap is not permission to treat a whole enforcement package as proven.

The HF side also needs stricter calibration. Baxter judged all four included trials high risk of bias. “No worsening” should usually be “no statistically detected worsening,” absent a non-inferiority design. Partial offset in one high-needs cost sample is not every HF program's economics. Barocas is a modeled displacement hazard, not a measured all-population death rate. The HUD figures are real trends, but the recent decline and supply/implementation context belong beside the longer increase. These qualifications strengthen the housing conclusion by keeping it within its evidence.

Newer evaluations were not searched comprehensively enough to certify the ledger as current. The review above fills one important gap; it does not license “nothing else relevant exists.” The revised conclusion should be: **HF has substantial evidence for housing stability in studied populations; treatment and recovery interventions have distinct evidence for distinct outcomes; the evidence assembled does not validate the entire Cicero/EO package or an individual family's alleged medication condition.**

### 6. Public naming: ethical and legal assessment

The sentence “she is an adult survivor speaking publicly under her own name, and withholding her account silences her” presents a false choice. Naming an adult who publicly filed a case can respect her expressed agency. It does not require copying every sensitive allegation into a public repository. Nor does operator authorization establish her consent to this specific amplification, permanence, classification or association with the larger framework.

For Annie, relevant risks include unwanted search prominence, harassment, repeated exposure of intimate history, third-party reinterpretation of her account, and identification of others mentioned in a complaint. Her public choice to speak weighs in favour of hearing and accurately attributing her account; it does not make those additional costs vanish. Minimizing unnecessary detail can preserve her agency without claiming that silence is protective.

For Sam and other named family members, the principal risk is readers receiving disputed allegations and inferred motives as established findings. “Distinct specimen,” “Retaliation,” and “the full move-set” are more consequential than the mere existence of the complaint. A living accused person and people whose alleged actions occurred while minors deserve accurate dates, status, denials and proportional detail. The same standard should apply to less powerful accused people.

Legally, a public filing does not provide universal immunity for republication. Fair-report protection, where applicable, concerns accurate and fair reporting of official material under the governing jurisdiction's rules; adopting additional defamatory factual implications is a separate question. *Medico v. Time* illustrates a jurisdiction-specific fair-report analysis, not a universal safe harbour for this repository. [Primary decision](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F2/643/134/454231/) Publication location, governing law, plaintiff status, precise wording and repository practices would be needed to assess actual exposure. The counterclaim's survival underscores that unresolved factual assertions should not be promoted to findings; it does not decide this repo's liability.

**Summary-level wording serves this ledger's stated purpose equally well.** The analytical subject is the family response's mental-health/money framing and disputed support conditions. That does not require graphic particulars of alleged childhood conduct. Use the attributed summary above, link the relevant public court record, include the denial close to the allegation, keep procedural status current, and distinguish observer analysis from claimant language. Restricted preservation of necessary detail and public minimization can coexist.
