# Survivor comparator: Dylan Farrow · Mackenzie Phillips ↔ Ann (Annie) Altman (2026-10-03)

*Operator-directed comparator for TD-009. Dylan Farrow is a similar case: a private person
who became public through her own testimony, about a powerful family member, given as an
adult about abuse she alleges she suffered as a child. Testimony comes first, per the standing
note. Allegations are recorded at summary level: what each woman has said publicly, as her
claim.*

**Provenance:** `[IN-FRAMEWORK]`, compiled by the proposer and not self-verified (Priority

statements are P2.

## 1. Dylan Farrow: her account first

- **The allegation.** She alleges that Woody Allen, her adoptive father, sexually assaulted her
  in August 1992 at the family's Connecticut home, when she was 7.
- **Her public testimony as an adult:**
  - an open letter, published through Nicholas Kristof's *New York Times* column (Feb 2014);
  - a first television interview (CBS, Jan 2018);
  - the HBO series *Allen v. Farrow* (Feb 2021).
- **Institutional record** (S1 relay):
  - **Connecticut prosecutor Frank Maco (Sept 1993):** said he had **"probable cause"** to
    prosecute, but declined to bring charges in order to spare the child a trial.
  - **Yale–New Haven Hospital clinic (1993):** concluded no abuse occurred. Its lead doctor's
    sworn statement theorized that Dylan was **"emotionally unstable"** and **coached** by her
    mother. Vanity Fair later reported that he had not personally interviewed Dylan, though
    the statement implied he had.
  - **Judge Elliott Wilk (NY custody case, 1993):** found **"no credible evidence"** that Mia
    Farrow coached Dylan, and called Allen "self-absorbed, untrustworthy and insensitive." He
    denied Allen visitation with Dylan. He found the abuse evidence **inconclusive**.
  - **Allen was never charged.** He has denied the allegation throughout.
- **The response:**
  - Allen's lawyer said the allegation was **"implanted"** by Mia Farrow (2014).
  - Moses Farrow, a sibling, published a 4,600-word essay in 2018 defending Allen and
    accusing Mia of abuse. Ronan Farrow, another sibling, supports Dylan.
  - **Institutional uptake:** Allen's memoir deal at Hachette (2020) was cancelled after an
    employee walkout. Another publisher released the memoir within weeks.

## 1b. Mackenzie Phillips: her account first *(third case, added at the operator's request)*

- **The allegation** (her 2009 memoir *High on Arrival* and an interview with Oprah Winfrey):
  her father, the rock musician John Phillips (d. 2001), raped her at 18 while she was
  incapacitated after a drug binge. A sexual relationship followed and lasted about a decade,
  including on the eve of her wedding at 19. She first called the later relationship
  "consensual." She grew up as a child star in a household awash with drugs.
- **The response** (S1 press relay):
  - Her **stepmother, Michelle Phillips**, said she was lying, "trying to cash in on her book,"
    had "a lot of mental illness," "a needle stuck up her arm for 35 years," and was "jealous of
    her siblings."
  - Her **half-sister, Chynna Phillips**, said Mackenzie had told her in 1997 and that she
    **believed her**, while saying she could not vouch for every detail.
- **Differences from the other two cases.** The alleged rape began when she was 18, not in
  childhood. The accused was **already dead**, so the response came from the family, not from
  the accused. Her addiction is documented, not merely alleged.

## 1c. Christina Crawford: near-match, a different kind of abuse

Her memoir *Mommie Dearest* (1978) alleged physical and emotional abuse, not sexual abuse, by
her adoptive mother, Joan Crawford, a film star. Joan's will cut her out "for reasons which are
well known to them." Her sisters Cathy and Cindy denounced the book and denied any abuse.
**Material lever plus sibling denial**, the Altman closure features, without the sexual
allegation and without pathologizing in the sources read. Recorded as a near-match, not a
same-shape case.

## 3. What the comparison does to TD-009's claims

- **"The reversed causal arrow is a recurring Move 3 form": upgraded to three instances**
  (Farrow, Phillips, Altman). **Phillips matches Altman's exact pair of disqualifiers,
  mental illness plus money motive**, and that pair came from the family, with the accused
  dead. So the move protects the family's account, not only the accused's own interest.
  **Recurrence of the form in three selected cases; no prevalence claim.** The form recurs
  across these cases. Whether the credibility attack *reverses* a true causal arrow is **U**
  in each case, Phillips explicitly so. What is observed is the **use of the testifier's
  mental state to discount the report**, which requires no finding on causation. *(Wording
  revised per independent review and its self-review: selection rules out a prevalence
  estimate, not the recurring form.)* **Selection warning (same as Priority 32):** these cases were chosen *because*
  they fit the shape. They show the form exists and recurs. They cannot show how often it
  happens. A base-rate test needs cases sampled without regard to the response.
  - *Upgrades if* further cases show distress treated as disqualifying while its alleged cause
    goes unexamined.
  - *Downgrades if* the disqualification turns out to rest, in these cases, on clinical
    evidence that rules out the alleged cause.
- *Earlier reading (two instances), kept for the record:*
  In both cases the testifier's psychological state was used to disqualify her, without
  ruling out that the alleged abuse caused it. In Dylan's case a judge **rejected** the
  related coaching theory, so the disqualification there met an independent check and partly
  failed. Two instances are still not a pattern. A third independent case is needed.
- **"The family dynamic is a very unique specimen" (operator): partly confirmed, partly
  narrowed.**
  - **Not unique:** the disqualification form. It recurs almost exactly in Farrow, and in
    Phillips with the same mental-illness-plus-money pair.
  - **Distinctive in Altman:** (1) **the whole family stands unanimously with the accused**, so
    there is no in-family corroborator; (2) **the testifier depends materially on the side
    that impeaches her**; (3) **the diamond**, care given in the giver's currency, set against
    a stated need for food. Farrow has none of the three. Phillips lacks
    (1), since her half-sister believed her, and (2) is undetermined. Crawford, the
    near-match, has (2) as the will and (1) as sibling denial. So **closure plus a material
    lever** also recurs, but in these sources only Altman combines it with the sexual
    allegation and a living, powerful accused. The comparison sharpens the
    operator's claim: what is unique is **closure and a material lever, held by the side of a
    living, powerful accused**, not the disqualification.
- **In this sample, only Farrow had an independent merits check.** Phillips had none
  because the accused was dead; Altman has none on the merits so far. *No rarity estimate:
  the sample has no denominator (per review).*
- **Farrow shows what Altman lacks: an independent check.** A prosecutor and a judge looked at
  the case from outside the family, and one rejected the accused side's central theory. That
  is the ledger's general thesis at household scale: harm tracks the absence of an
  *independent* check (comparison doc §4.1; H10).
